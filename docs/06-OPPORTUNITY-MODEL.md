# 06 — Opportunity Model

## Opportunity output

Every opportunity should answer six questions:

1. **Who** has the problem?
2. **What** problem are they trying to solve?
3. **How strong** is the evidence?
4. **What alternatives** already exist?
5. **What small product** could solve it?
6. **Why is it worth validating now?**

## Required sections

### Problem
A concrete user problem, not a generic market description.

### Target user
A specific user segment and context of use.

### Evidence
A list of supporting signals with source, date, URL/identifier where available, and evidence type.

### Existing solutions
Known alternatives, their positioning, pricing where reliable, and observable gaps.

### Product hypothesis
A narrow product concept that can be tested without building a large platform.

### MVP
The smallest feature set required to test the core value proposition.

### Monetization
A hypothesis for who pays, what they pay for, and a plausible pricing mechanism.

### Risks
At minimum:

- evidence quality risk;
- competition risk;
- acquisition risk;
- technical/data dependency risk;
- monetization risk.

### Validation plan
A concrete experiment that can produce real evidence before significant development investment.

## Recommendation states

- `candidate`
- `needs_validation`
- `approved_for_mvp`
- `building`
- `launched`
- `validated`
- `rejected`

The state represents the product lifecycle and is separate from the numerical opportunity score.
